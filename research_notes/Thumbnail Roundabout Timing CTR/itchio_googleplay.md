# itch.io covers and Google Play listing graphics for Roundabout Timing

Research date: 2026-10-08. Context: one-tap roundabout/traffic-timing game, mobile portrait + browser, English only, current itch.io-style CTR 1.0%. "Verified" = stated on an official Google or itch.io page (or by itch.io's admin leafo). Everything else is community or third-party and labelled as such.

## itch.io: cover image size, ratio, GIF, cropping

### Takeaway
Recommended cover is 630x500 (ratio 1.26:1), minimum 315x250, max 3 MB, up to 3840x2160. Animated GIFs are allowed, but they show the first frame and animate only on hover. The browse grid renders about 315x250 and crops the centre, so keep the subject centred and test at that size. Official itch.io admin guidance on CTR is "anything above 1% is good" (2018), so 1.0% is at the low end of good.

### Cited Findings
- itch.io's Quality Guidelines say: "The cover image is typically the first element someone sees when they discover your game on our browse pages. If you don't have one, they'll likely skip over it." Animated GIFs are allowed, but seizure-inducing effects must be avoided. — [itch.io Quality Guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines)
- The official page-design doc says the banner "is probably the most important graphic part of your page", and that banners can be semi-transparent PNGs or animated GIFs. It gives no dimensions. Note: that is the page banner, not the cover. — [itch.io page design docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/design)
- Recommended cover dimensions are 630x500 px, maximum 3840x2160 px, maximum file size 3 MB. The source is a community guide ("Itch Game Page Image Guide and Templates", hedgiespresso) that reproduces the Edit Game page field text. This is secondary, not itch.io docs. The thread notes that 630x500 (1.26:1) differs from 3840x2160 (1.78:1), so scaling to max without keeping the ratio causes unintended cropping. — [hedgiespresso template page](https://hedgiespresso.itch.io/itch-page-image-templates) and [itch.io forum post](https://itch.io/post/5308783)
- The admin (leafo) recommended 630x500 in a cover-GIF thread. — [itch.io forum post 14390512](https://itch.io/post/14390512)
- Minimum cover size 315x250 and recommended 630x500. Browse renders every cover as a 315x250 centre crop (per a third-party launch guide, 2026). A community member (Pixelkiln, not staff) showed a 1179x507 cover being centre-cropped so a car's rear bumper was cut off, and advised re-exporting at 630x500. Treat the centre-crop mechanics as community/third-party, not official docs. — [Cinevva itch.io launch guide (2026)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide) and [itch.io forum post 17220956](https://itch.io/post/17220956)
- GIF covers are "hover to play". Admin leafo: "Cover images have been 'hover to play' for many years now." A bug had briefly made them autoplay, and it was fixed. So the static first frame is what gets judged in the grid. — [itch.io forum thread (leafo)](https://itch.io/post/14457703/view-in-topic)
- A community devlog recommends putting the static cover art as frame 1 of the GIF, then the gameplay frames, at 630x500 (tools: ScreenToGif, ezgif). — [Kleebuu devlog](https://dylanbrowngames.itch.io/kleebuu-craves-fruit-salad/devlog/329834/how-to-make-cover-image-with-art-gameplay)
- If a project has screenshots uploaded, the cover does not appear in the screenshot area unless it is also uploaded there. — [hedgiespresso guide](https://hedgiespresso.itch.io/itch-page-image-templates)
- A community report says a very large GIF is penalised on browse for bandwidth reasons, but I could not retrieve the primary admin statement. Keep GIFs well under 3 MB (itch.io's stated limit). — search snippet only, see Gaps
- Cover design advice from the Cinevva guide (third party, 2026): make it legible when small, test at thumbnail scale, use bold colours and a clear focal point, convey art style/mood/genre cues, and include a readable title. — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/itch-io-launch-guide)

### itch.io CTR benchmarks and what gets clicks
- Admin leafo (22 Feb 2018): "The games on the top of our browse pages have a CTR around .6 to 1.3." CTR varies by placement: homepage impressions convert lower than tag-matched browse impressions. His two recommendations are a good cover image (with a GIF if applicable) and using all classification options so the game appears in more searches. — [itch.io thread "new analytics ctr context"](https://itch.io/t/197172/new-analytics-ctr-context) (2018, old)
- Admin leafo (Aug 2018): "anything above 1% is good, and having 3% is really good." Higher impressions tend to mean lower CTR. Developers in the thread report 0.5% to 6%. One reports improved thumbnail art raised CTR from 0.3% to 2.6% (anecdote, unverified). Another reports a very low 0.36% CTR and updated the cover (anecdote). — [itch.io thread "whats your ctr whats a good ctr"](https://itch.io/t/278399/whats-your-ctr-whats-a-good-ctr)
- itch.io's own analytics define CTR as clicks divided by impressions, per placement. — [itch.io updates: project analytics](https://itch.io/updates/updates-to-project-analytics-filtering-collections-impressions-and-more)

### Inferences
- At 1.0% you sit around the top-of-browse range (0.6-1.3%) and at the admin's "good" threshold. A big jump needs either better placement or a clearly better cover. The 2018 numbers are old and traffic mix has changed, so use them only as a rough yardstick.
- For a 630x500 slot shown at about 315x250: design on 630x500, and keep the logo and the key subject inside the middle region. Frame 1 of any GIF must be the "sell" frame because the grid shows it static until hover.
- Portrait mobile screenshots do not fit a landscape-ish 1.26:1 cover. Compose a purpose-made cover (roundabout seen from above, cars, bold logo) rather than cropping a phone screenshot.

### Gaps
- No official itch.io doc found that states the 315x250 centre-crop behaviour. It rests on a third-party guide and a community post.
- The itch.io docs pages for cover size (`/docs/creators/pages` returned 404). The 630x500 / 3 MB spec comes from the in-form text as reproduced by a community page and from the admin recommending 630x500.
- Could not view itch.io's browse pages for named top web racing/traffic game covers: itch.io served a Cloudflare human-verification check to the browser tool, and I did not attempt to bypass it. No named itch.io examples are therefore reported.
- No hard data (A/B) on how GIF covers change itch.io CTR. The "GIF measurably lifts clicks" claim in the Cinevva guide is unsourced.

## Google Play: exact requirements and policy

### Takeaway
Icon: 512x512 32-bit PNG with alpha, max 1024 KB. Feature graphic: 1024x500 JPEG or 24-bit PNG without alpha. Screenshots: min 2, max 8 per device type, JPEG/24-bit PNG, 16:9 or 9:16, for games at least three at 1920x1080 or 1080x1920 minimum. Google bans ranking/award/price/promo claims, "Download now" style calls to action, and caps screenshot taglines at 20% of the image.

### Cited Findings
- Icon: 32-bit PNG with alpha, 512x512 px, max 1024 KB. Do not include "badges or text that suggest ranking, price, Google Play categories, or otherwise mislead users". — [Play Console Help: Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151)
- Feature graphic: JPEG or 24-bit PNG (no alpha), 1024x500 px. Keep prominent visuals and the focal point towards the centre. Do not put key elements (brand logo, app name, primary slogan, main UI) in the cutoff zones, because they "may be cut off depending on the UI format". Google recommends more vibrant colours, avoiding pure white or dark grey (to avoid blending with the Play background), and putting background elements at the edges. — [Play Console Help: Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151)
- Feature graphic content rules (same page): no content that "reflects or suggests Google Play performance, ranking, user testimonials, accolades or awards, or price and promotional information"; no time-sensitive content; avoid fine details that vanish on phones; do not duplicate the app icon as prominent branding; alt text required. — [Play Console Help: Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151) (first read of page; exact wording of some items summarised by the fetch tool)
- Where the feature graphic appears: as the cover image of the preview video, in large-format app collections "including ads", and in large-format recommended game groups with videos/screenshots. Some formats add UI overlays, and not following the focal-point/cutoff guidance may make the listing ineligible for those formats. — [Play Console Help: Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151); the ineligibility sentence is relayed by a search snippet of the same page
- Screenshots: minimum two to publish, maximum 8 per device type, JPEG or 24-bit PNG (no alpha), each side 320-3840 px, the longer side cannot be more than twice the shorter. 16:9 landscape (min 1920x1080) or 9:16 portrait (min 1080x1920). Games: at least three screenshots showing the in-game experience. Screenshots must show the real in-app/in-game experience; taglines limited to 20% of the image area; no "Download now"-type calls to action; no ranking/awards/testimonials/price/promo content; alt text required. — [Play Console Help: Add preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151)
- Metadata/store-listing policy: short description 80 chars, full 4,000, title 30. Avoid "Free"/"No Ads" style promo words in the title. Minimise text overlays in screenshots; provide localised screenshots when they contain text. Keep key elements centred for multiple screen sizes. — [Google Play help: store listing and promotion policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723)
- Live check (screenshot of Play Store web, 8 Oct 2026, search "traffic game" in the US storefront): search results render the feature graphic as a large 16:9-ish card (about 1.78:1, so the 2.05:1 asset is cropped at the sides) with the icon, title, developer, rating below it, and a play-button overlay when a video exists. Cards without a feature graphic show a blank grey box. Observed first-hand, not from documentation.
- Live check, Traffic Escape! detail page on Play web (8 Oct 2026): the hero area shows a centred portrait-style image/video cover on a dark background, with title, developer, rating and install button on the left. Screenshots appear in a row directly under it. Observed first-hand.
- Display change history: Google removed the feature graphic from the top of the app page in April 2018 (it had been the top third), moving icon/rating/install to the top and video/screenshots to the same area. The "up to 30% of conversions" figure is a claim in the Gummicube article, no data shown. — [Gummicube (2018)](https://gummicube.com/blog/is-this-the-end-of-google-plays-feature-graphic) (old, dated)
- Hero content carousel: Google announced at I/O 2025 that from July 2025 all developers can add a hero content carousel (a slider with three images instead of one) and a YouTube playlist carousel. AppTweak notes the carousel pushes screenshots and the short description below the fold of the first screen. A/B test availability for the carousel was unclear. — [Android Developers Blog: I/O 2025 what's new in Google Play](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/05/io-2025-whats-new-in-google-play.html); [AppTweak I/O 2025 ASO updates](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-i-o-2025-aso-updates)
- Third-party design rules for the feature graphic: a roughly 716x350 centred "safe zone" is cited by one site and 924x400 by another (conflicting, neither official); a play-button overlay of roughly 96x96 px sits dead-centre when a promo video exists; the graphic is cropped between 16:9 and 4:3 in some collection formats. — [trysonar.app](https://trysonar.app/blog/google-play-feature-graphic) and [applaunchflow](https://www.applaunchflow.com/blog/how-to-make-play-store-feature-graphic-2026) (low-quality third-party, treat as heuristics)

### Inferences
- Design the feature graphic as one centred idea (logo plus roundabout/cars) in roughly the middle 700x350, no text near the horizontal centre if a video is attached, nothing important in the outer ~150 px each side. My own live check confirms side cropping to about 16:9 in search results.
- A portrait one-tap game should upload portrait 9:16 screenshots (at least 1080x1920, ideally 3 to 8). Short captions are allowed, but keep them under 20% of the image and never use "Download now", "#1", "Best game", "Free", award or rating badges.
- No official statement gives the number of screenshots visible above the fold; in the live check the first four portrait screenshots are visible in the detail page row.

### Gaps
- I could not locate Google's official Play Academy / "store listing best practices" page with conversion advice beyond the policy page. `developer.android.com/distribute/best-practices/launch/store-listing` redirects to a Play Console marketing page whose content I could not retrieve.
- Official hero content carousel size/aspect requirements were not found.
- Exact wording of the feature-graphic cutoff-zone diagram (dimensions) is not in what I retrieved.

## Top casual/hypercasual driving and traffic games on Google Play: patterns

### Takeaway
Observed on the Play web search for "traffic game" (8 Oct 2026): feature graphics lean on a giant, glossy 3D-lettered logotype over a bright cartoon city scene, saturated blue/yellow/green palettes, a top-down gridlock of cars, and cartoon characters (police). Screenshots in the leader (Traffic Escape!, 10M+ downloads, 4.5 stars) are large gameplay with a bottom caption and a bright yellow car hero.

### Cited Findings (first-hand observation from live Play pages, 8 Oct 2026, unless linked otherwise)
- Search results for "traffic game" (US) showed feature graphics for: Bus Traffic Fever! (GOODROID; huge glossy blue-and-yellow 3D logo over a sunny city, no gameplay UI); Traffic Puzzle: Car Jam Escape (Huuuge Games; chunky white "TRAFFIC" with yellow "puzzle" lettering, two cartoon police characters, bright blue/teal/magenta); Traffic 3D Parking: Escape Jam (Pleasure City; logo over a top-down lot of yellow cars); Traffic Racer (skgames; realistic car in motion, motion blur); Traffic Rider (skgames; close-up yellow motorbike); Traffic Frenzy: Road Jam (Little Whale; top-down aerial view of a dense gridlocked intersection, yellow taxis); Traffix: Traffic Simulator (Infinity Games; minimalist pink/purple road lines on light grey, the opposite of the glossy style); Traffic Escape: Rush hour game (Cronoprice; red background, bold yellow "TRAFFIC ESCAPE" with a "NO ADS" badge); Traffic Escape! (FOMO Games; a gameplay clip with black letterboxing).
- Traffic Escape! (FOMO Games, 10M+ downloads, 4.5 stars, 1.13M reviews): icon is a saturated green/purple border with a yellow taxi in the centre. Screenshots are portrait gameplay on bright green grass with a top-down grid of yellow cars, each with a bold white caption at the bottom ("CLEAR THE TRAFFIC", "TRY NEW SKINS", "USE NEW ELEMENTS", "CHARGE THE TRUCK") and a large yellow car at the bottom corner. Detail page captured via browser, 8 Oct 2026.
- Icons in the same search: Bus Traffic Fever! uses a cartoon bus packed with colourful characters, Traffic Escape! a single yellow car, Traffix a dark tile with a stylised junction line, Traffic Puzzle a cartoon character.
- General hypercasual icon guidance from Supersonic (Unity, with CVR figures): high contrast; show the core mechanic (Emoji Puzzle +30% CVR by showing the connecting line; Tall Man Run +15% by showing gameplay elements); simplify and enlarge the hero object (Get the Supercar +22% CVR with a larger car and gates; Perfect Lie +10% by removing text/objects); cartoon style and recognisable emojis (String Art +8%); a darker outline (My Mini Mart +7%). The data are Supersonic's own tests, publication date not shown on the page. — [Supersonic: 5 design tips for hyper-casual icons](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/5-design-tips-for-hyper-casual-icons-to-improve-your-app-store-cvr/)
- AppTweak hypercasual ASO guidance: icon shows key gameplay and bright colours; avoid characters unless instantly recognisable (Angry Birds); screenshots show clean graphics, match the gameplay orientation, prioritise the first 3; Going Balls cited for clear gameplay with level captions. Dated 2021 data. — [AppTweak: ASO tips for hyper-casual games](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/aso-tips-for-hyper-casual-mobile-games) (older)
- Colour: darker colours such as black or brown tend to raise CPI; bright cartoon colours perform better (hypercasual creatives, Supersonic/Game Developer). — [Game Developer: hypercasual colours](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-to-choose-hypercasual-game-colors-5-beginner-tips)

### Inferences
- The traffic category is saturated with "traffic jam / escape" puzzle games that use top-down gridlock, yellow cars and glossy 3D logos. A timing game about a roundabout can differentiate by showing the roundabout ring with cars at the merge moment and a single tap cue, but should still adopt the genre's visual language: saturated colours, huge readable logo, one hero vehicle.
- Fahrzeugfarben are game information in this project (CLAUDE.md), so do not recolour cars arbitrarily in marketing art; use the real vehicle types.
- Minimalist cards like Traffix show another route: a distinctive flat, high-contrast road-line look stands out amid glossy art. It is a priced game ($2.49), so it is not a direct comparison.

### Gaps
- No ranking data (top chart positions) was retrievable; "top-performing" is judged by download tier and search placement only, from one query. Roughly half of the search cards loaded without images (lazy loading), so the sample is about 12 visible feature graphics.
- Did not capture hypercasual driving games outside the "traffic" query (e.g. Crossy-style arcade driving, "Highway"/"Drift" titles).
- No fresh per-game CTR or CVR data.

## Published conversion and CTR benchmarks (icon vs feature graphic vs screenshots)

### Takeaway
Reliable public numbers for the relative weight of icon vs feature graphic vs screenshots barely exist. Per-asset uplifts come from vendor case studies and secondary blogs. Google's own tool (store listing experiments) measures installs per visitor, not browse-level CTR, and Play Console shows listing conversion versus peer group.

### Cited Findings
- AppTweak: average Google Play store visitor-to-installer conversion rate in the US was 16.15% in 2025 across all categories; Games-Strategy the lowest at 6.6%. Published 18 May 2026. — [AppTweak: Average app conversion rate per category](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category-2019)
- A search summary attributed to AppTweak "Racing games convert at 20.6%", and another "27.3% in H1 2024" average. I could not find these on the AppTweak page itself (the page covers only Strategy and Trivia among games), so treat as unverified. — see page above; unverified
- Secondary blog (trysonar.app, updated 1 Oct 2026, citing StoreMaven/SplitMetrics/Phiture without links): Play games CVR 25-40%; icon influences "up to 17%" of users in search; "screenshots account for up to 45% of the decision"; captions on screenshots +25% on average. These are unsourced aggregates, treat as anecdotal. — [trysonar.app](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-store-conversion-rate.md)
- Google Play store listing experiments can test icon, feature graphic, screenshots, promo video and descriptions; the metric is installs per store listing visitor. — [Play Console store listing experiments (archived Android Developers page)](https://web-archive.nli.org.il/National_Library/oe_/https://developer.android.com/distribute/best-practices/grow/store-listing-experiments) (archive, older)
- Third-party summary: 57% of top games on Google Play A/B tested screenshots at least twice in 2024 versus 34% of non-games; icon tests produce the largest swings (published ranges 20-30%, median winning icon lift 8-12% per an AppTweak-attributed summary). The underlying AppTweak report was not retrieved. — [tms-outsource summary](https://tms-outsource.com/blog/posts/google-play-store-listing-experiments/) (low quality, unverified)
- SplitMetrics case studies: Ciliz (Spin the Bottle) +19% conversion from icon optimisation; Nanobit (Tabou Stories) +18% organic conversion from a new icon; Lab Cave (Love & Dating Story) 314% more Google Play installs with icon/screenshot testing. Vendor marketing, single cases. — [Ciliz](https://splitmetrics.com/cases/ciliz-improves-conversion-with-splitmetrics-agency/), [Nanobit](https://splitmetrics.com/cases/nanobit-increases-conversion-with-splitmetrics/), [Lab Cave](https://splitmetrics.com/cases/lab-cave-case-study-314-increase-in-installs-with-splitmetrics-a-b-testing/)
- Gummicube (2018): "the feature graphic accounts for up to 30% of all conversions" (before its 2018 demotion, no data shown). — [Gummicube](https://gummicube.com/blog/is-this-the-end-of-google-plays-feature-graphic) (2018, outdated)

### Inferences
- With the feature graphic now mostly a video cover and large-format placement (search cards in my live check), it matters most where it is shown big: search and browse cards. The icon and the first screenshots matter on every surface.
- The icon-led gains in the Supersonic and SplitMetrics cases come from simplifying and showing the mechanic. That fits a one-tap roundabout game: icon = a single car on a roundabout ring with high contrast.

### Gaps
- No published itch.io-vs-Play-style CTR benchmarks specific to hypercasual driving games.
- No verifiable direct comparison of icon vs feature graphic vs screenshot CTR/CVR from AppTweak, SplitMetrics, StoreMaven, Phiture or MobileAction. Most numbers are second-hand.
- Google Play Console's own peer-group benchmark figures are not public.
